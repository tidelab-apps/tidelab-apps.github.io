Turn one page of exam notes into five questions

From the small team at Tidelab that makes Decklet · 9 November 2026

If your exam is close, start with a small section of your own notes. Write questions you can answer before looking, rather than copying a paragraph onto the back of one card. Here is a five-card biology example you can adapt.

Start with a short note

Example note: In plant cells, photosynthesis takes place in chloroplasts. Chlorophyll absorbs light. The process uses carbon dioxide and water to make sugars and releases oxygen.

A prompt such as Explain photosynthesis asks for several things at once. For this first deck, separate the process, gases, pigment and location into questions you can check individually.

The resulting first deck

These are original introductory examples, not a complete syllabus. Check terminology against your course notes and add questions about the parts your course expects you to explain.

front,back
What process uses light energy to make sugars in plants?,Photosynthesis
Which gas do plants take in for photosynthesis?,Carbon dioxide
Which gas is released during oxygenic photosynthesis?,Oxygen
What green pigment absorbs light for photosynthesis?,Chlorophyll
In which organelle does photosynthesis occur in plant cells?,Chloroplast

Import and try a session

  1. Download the example CSV below or make your own two-column file. Each row should have one question and its answer.
  2. Open Decklet, choose Import, then CSV or TSV, and select the file. Turn on First row is a header.
  3. Check the preview: five cards found, no skipped rows. Name the deck Biology: photosynthesis and tap Import.
  4. Read a question and try to answer before revealing the back. Rate your actual recall. If you missed the gas question, check that note again and try explaining it in your own words.
  5. Use later review sessions for cards that are due. Keep room for practice problems and longer explanations that a short fact card cannot cover.

When the exam is close

Choose a manageable topic and check every answer against your material. If a card is vague, rewrite the question. A first session can show which answers you missed; it cannot promise an exam result or replace practising the kind of questions your exam uses.

No scan or subscription needed

This workflow uses manual text and CSV import. Those features and spaced repetition are free in Decklet without daily limits or ads; no account is needed. AI creation is an optional Pro feature and is not used here. Decklet requires an iPhone running iOS 26 or later.
